Julia Martin, a 32-year-old female at approximately 14 weeks’ gestation, presents for an antenatal check-up with reports of persistent fatigue, occasional palpitations, and frequent headaches. She reveals a history of heavy alcohol use, admitting to two to three drinks most evenings prior to learning about the pregnancy. Despite efforts to reduce her intake, she still experiences difficulty abstaining completely. She denies any acute abdominal pain, vaginal bleeding, or significant nausea and vomiting.

Her past medical history is notable for hypothyroidism, diagnosed five years ago. She has been on levothyroxine 100 mcg daily but admits to inconsistent adherence over the past few months. She attributes her irregular medication use to a busy schedule and forgetfulness. She denies any significant family history of thyroid disease, though her mother did experience early menopause and issues with low bone density. Julia has no known renal or hepatic disorders, but her heavy alcohol use raises concerns about potential liver strain during the pregnancy. She reports no known drug allergies.

On physical examination, Julia’s blood pressure is 118/72 mmHg, heart rate is 88 bpm, and temperature is 36.8°C. She appears fatigued but not acutely distressed. Thyroid palpation reveals no goiter or significant nodules, and fetal heart tones are present at a rate of 150 bpm via Doppler. Her obstetric examination is otherwise within normal limits.

Laboratory evaluations show a mildly elevated TSH of 4.8 mIU/L (current pregnancy reference range upper limit ~2.5–3 mIU/L), suggesting her hypothyroidism is not optimally controlled. Free T4 levels are at the lower end of the normal range, consistent with a need for dose adjustment. Although her liver function tests are within acceptable ranges for pregnancy, they will be closely monitored due to her reported alcohol intake. Further imaging or referral to a hepatologist may be warranted if abnormal enzymes develop.

Given her history of hypothyroidism, Julia requires close monitoring of thyroid function throughout the pregnancy. An increased levothyroxine dose, compliance reinforcement, and continued counseling regarding complete alcohol cessation are integral to her prenatal care. Support services and nutritional counseling will be provided to help reduce alcohol use and optimize fetal outcomes. Follow-up visits are arranged every four weeks to reassess maternal well-being, fetal development, and thyroid function.
