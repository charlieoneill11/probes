Maria Espinoza, a 47-year-old female, presents with a six-month history of generalized fatigue, occasional dizziness, and increased shortness of breath on exertion. She reports a significant alcohol intake, consuming three to four standard drinks most evenings for the past several years. Her dietary habits include frequent fast-food meals and minimal fresh produce, raising concerns about her cardiovascular risk.

On examination, Maria’s BMI is 29.1, placing her in the overweight category. Blood pressure is mildly elevated at 146/90 mmHg, and her heart rate is regular at 84 bpm. She has no current signs of acute distress. She denies chest pain, leg swelling, or palpitations but admits to feeling persistently drained despite adequate rest. There is no mention of pregnancy, and a recent serum beta-hCG test was negative. She has no known renal impairment, reporting normal urinary function and no history of chronic kidney disease.

Laboratory investigations reveal a fasting lipid profile with a total cholesterol of 245 mg/dL (high), LDL cholesterol of 170 mg/dL (elevated), and HDL cholesterol of 37 mg/dL (low). These findings suggest dyslipidemia, and Maria confirms she has not previously been on a statin or any other lipid-lowering therapy. Liver function tests show a mildly elevated ALT, although bilirubin and alkaline phosphatase remain within normal limits. She denies any recent symptoms of acute liver disease, such as severe right upper quadrant pain or jaundice. However, given her heavy alcohol use, ongoing liver enzyme monitoring is recommended. Thyroid function tests indicate a slightly elevated TSH, raising the possibility of subclinical hypothyroidism but without overt clinical manifestations at this time.

In reviewing treatment options, atorvastatin is typically the first-line statin recommended to address her elevated LDL levels and overall high cardiovascular risk. Before initiating therapy, it is prudent to confirm that she does not have active liver disease, ensure her thyroid function is adequately managed, and consider the impact of heavy alcohol consumption on hepatic metabolism. While statins are contraindicated in conditions such as acute liver disease, significantly elevated liver enzymes, pregnancy, severe renal impairment, or uncontrolled hypothyroidism, Maria’s clinical picture appears suitable for a low to moderate dose. The plan is to start atorvastatin 20 mg daily once her current laboratory values are stabilized and her lifestyle factors—particularly alcohol intake—are addressed to reduce the likelihood of adverse effects.
